* The relational model counts something
This talk is about the /relational semantics/ of the \lambda-calculus.

Relational semantics grew from Girard's /quantitative semantics/ [cite/na:@GirardJY:norfps]
and can be seen as a simple model of linear logic [cite//c:@GirardJY:linl].

It is easiest to present as a system of /non-idempotent intersection types/ (multi types) [cite//c:@DeCarvalhoD:exetlt]: the denotation of a term is the set of its typings
\[
 x_1: \mu_1, \ldots, x_n:\mu_n \vdash M: \alpha
\]

** Multi types

\begin{array}{rcll}
  \alpha  & ::=  & a \mid \mu \rightarrow \alpha \\
  \mu  & ::=  & [\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n] & n \geq 0\\
\end{array}

\[
\frac{}{x:[\alpha] \vdash x:\alpha}~\text{ax} \quad\quad
\frac{\Gamma_i \vdash M: \alpha_i~~(i \in I~\text{finite})}{\uplus_{i \in I}\Gamma_i \vdash M: [\alpha_i]_{i \in I}}~\text{many}
\]

\[
\frac{\Gamma, x:\mu \vdash M: \alpha}{\Gamma \vdash \lambda x.M: \mu \rightarrow \alpha}~\lambda
\quad\quad
\frac{\Gamma \vdash M: \mu \rightarrow \alpha \quad \Delta \vdash N: \mu}
{\Gamma \uplus \Delta \vdash MN : \alpha}~\text{@}
\]

A term may have many types: types are /elements of the model/, not traditional types.

** Quantitative aspects

This model has /quantitative/ content: the multisets count something. But what?
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
They keep track of how many times an argument is used: for example we can type
\[
f: [[\alpha] \rightarrow [\alpha'] \rightarrow \alpha] \vdash \lambda x. f~x~x:[\alpha, \alpha']\rightarrow \alpha.
\]
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
The /many/ rule means that these multiplicities are also seen in /derivations/.
\[
 \cfrac{\cfrac{\vdots}{f: [[\alpha] \rightarrow [\alpha'] \rightarrow \alpha] \vdash \lambda x. f~x~x:[\alpha, \alpha']\rightarrow \alpha}\quad \cfrac{\vdash M: \alpha \quad \vdash M: \alpha'}{\vdash M: [\alpha,\alpha']}}{f: [[\alpha] \rightarrow [\alpha'] \rightarrow \alpha]\vdash (\lambda x. f~x~x) M: \alpha }~@
\]
The function uses \(M\) twice, so we need /two/ derivations of \(M\).
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
Though substitution may duplicate or discard /terms/, it does not duplicate or discard their /type derivations/.

#+begin_theorem
\(M\) has a type if and only if \(M\) has a head normal form; and the number of @-rules in a derivation bounds the length of the head reduction.
#+end_theorem

** B\ouml{}hm preorder up to extensionality
We also define two extensions:
 -  \(M \leBconv N\) means that the B\ouml{}hm tree of \(M\) is contained in that of \(N\) /up to (possibly infinite) \eta-conversion/
 -  \(M \leBred N\) means that the B\ouml{}hm tree of \(M\) is contained in that of \(N\) /up to (possibly infinite) \eta-reduction/

\[
 x \not  \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}} \lambda y. xy \quad  \lambda y. xy \not \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}} x
\]
\[
 x \leBconv \lambda y. xy \quad  \lambda y. xy  \leBconv x
\]
\[
 x \not \leBred \lambda y. xy \quad  \lambda y. xy \leBred x
\]

#+begin_theorem
(Hyland, Wadsworth) Taking the contextual preorder that observes head normal forms,
\[
 M \lectx N \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad M \leBconv N
\]
and both coincide with the order induced by Scott's \(D_\infty\) models.
#+end_theorem

#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
 - \(\Leftarrow\): via \(D_\infty\): \(M \leBconv N\) implies \(\sem{M} \sqsubseteq \sem{N}\), and the model is compositional and adequate (\(\sem{M} \neq \bot\) iff \(M\) has a hnf).
 - \(\Rightarrow\): the "B\ouml{}hm-out" technique [cite//c:@HylandJME:synces] builds a context that distinguishes terms with different B\ouml{}hm trees:
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)   
\[
 M \not \leBconv N \Rightarrow \exists C[-]. C[M] \rightarrow^*\mathsf{hnf}~\text{but}~C[N]\not\rightarrow^*\mathsf{hnf}
\]

** Understanding the relational preorder

Using adequacy (typable iff head normalising) and compositionality:
 \[ \text{if}~\sem{M} \subseteq \sem{N}~\text{then for all}~C[-]. C[M]\rightarrow^* \mathsf{hnf} \Rightarrow C[N]\rightarrow^* \mathsf{hnf}\]
i.e. \(M \lectx N\), so by Hyland--Wadsworth the denotational order implies the extensional B\ouml{}hm-tree order
 \(M \leBconv N\).

The converse is not true. For example \(x \leBconv \lambda y. x y \) but
\[
 x : [a] \vdash x: a \quad\quad   x : [a] \not\vdash \lambda y. x y: a \quad
\]

#+reveal: split

In fact thanks to [cite/t:@BreuvartF:relgmw] we know that
\[
\sem{M} \subseteq \sem{N} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad M \leBred N
\]

But this is /qualitative/. Where have the quantities gone?

Can we recover a contextual ordering by paying attention to quantities?

* Improvement

[cite/t:@SandsD:impta] introduced the idea of a contextual /improvement/ order.

Idea: observe not just what happens but how long it takes.

For example: observe the /length/ of a head-reduction sequence.

We write \( M \Downarrow_h^k \) if \(M\) reduces to hnf in \(k\) head reduction steps.

Then we can define /improvement/: \(M \lesands N \) iff
\[
 \text{for all contexts}~C[-]. C[M]\Downarrow_h^k \Rightarrow \exists k'\leq k. C[N]\Downarrow_h^{k'}
\]

\(N\) does the same things as \(M\) in fewer steps.

** Improvement is not \beta-invariant

\[
 (\lambda x.x)\,y \Downarrow_h^1 \qquad\qquad y \Downarrow_h^0
\]
so \(y \not\lesands (\lambda x.x)\,y\), although they are \beta-equal.

An order that counts reductions /cannot/ be the order induced by a denotational semantics that is sound for \beta-reduction.

(There are very few denotational models of improvement orders as a result. One exception is Ghica's /slot games/ [cite/na:@GhicaDR:slog], which expose every internal step through a global counter.)

#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
So our plan to use this idea to capture the relational order must fail. Yet here we are...

* Main result
#+begin_theorem
For \lambda-terms \(M, N\), the following are equivalent:
 1. \(M \leBred N\) (B\ouml{}hm trees up to \eta-reduction)
 2. \(M \lepwc N\) (a refined, annotated relational semantics)
 3. \(M \leimp N\) (/interaction improvement/: a quantitative contextual preorder)
 4. \(M \lerel N\), i.e. \(\sem{M} \subseteq \sem{N}\) in the relational model
#+end_theorem

#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
A /quantitative/ Hyland--Wadsworth theorem: the relational model's preorder is an improvement order, counting interactions.

* The checkers calculus
[cite/t:@AccattoliB:inte]: colour every abstraction and application black \(\bullet\) or white \(\circ\).

Intuition: the program (player) is black, the context (opponent) is white. The calculus is entirely symmetric.
\[
 M, N ::= x \mid \lambda_\bullet x. M \mid \lambda_\circ x. M \mid M \bullet N \mid M \circ N
\]

Silent reductions (colours match):
\[
 (\lambda_\bullet x.M)\bullet N \rightarrow M[N/x]  \quad  (\lambda_\circ x.M)\circ N \rightarrow M[N/x]
\]

Interaction reductions (colours differ):
\[
 (\lambda_\bullet x.M)\circ N \stackrel{\interact}{\rightarrow} M[N/x]  \quad  (\lambda_\circ x.M)\bullet N \stackrel{\interact}{\rightarrow} M[N/x]
\]

We can embed the ordinary \lambda-calculus by painting everything \(\bullet\). Every reduction is then silent.

The fun starts when we embed a \(\bullet\)-painted term in a \(\circ\)-painted context.

** Interaction improvement

We observe the /number of interaction steps/ in a head-reduction sequence.

We write \( M \Downarrow_{\interact}^k \) if \(M\) reduces to hnf with \(k\) head interaction steps (silent steps are free).

Then we define /interaction improvement/: \(M \leimp N \) iff
\[
 \text{for all checkers contexts}~C[-]. C[M]\Downarrow_{\interact}^k \Rightarrow \exists k'\leq k. C[N]\Downarrow_{\interact}^{k'}
\]

For \lambda-terms, \(M \leimp N\) means \(M^\bullet \leimp N^\bullet\) (paint them black; quantify over all checkers contexts).

#+reveal: split

The /interaction preorder/ \(\leint\) [cite//c:@AccattoliB:inte] asks instead for /exactly/ the same \(k\).

\[
 (\lambda_\bullet x.x) \circ (\lambda_\circ x.x) \;\leimp\; \lambda_\circ x.x
 \qquad\text{but}\qquad
 (\lambda_\bullet x.x) \circ (\lambda_\circ x.x) \;\not\leint\; \lambda_\circ x.x
\]
Same head normal form, but the left-hand side needs one more interaction to reach it.

** \eta-reduction is an improvement

For a regular \lambda-term painted \(\bullet\) throughout, /all steps are silent/: \beta-equivalent terms are equivalent in \(\leimp\).

But \(\circ\)-painted contexts distinguish \eta-convertible terms, and \eta-reduction delivers improvement:
\[
 \lambda_\bullet x. \lambda_\bullet y. x \bullet y \;\leimp\; \lambda_\bullet x. x
 \qquad
 \lambda_\bullet x. x \;\not\leimp\; \lambda_\bullet x. \lambda_\bullet y. x \bullet y
\]

#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
The first of these was only a conjecture in [cite/t:@AccattoliB:inte]: it is a corollary of our main theorem.

* Checkers semantics

To complete the picture, we use a refined relational model.

We annotate the types:
\begin{array}{rcll}
  \alpha  & ::=  & a \mid \mu \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \alpha \mid \mu \stackrel{\circ}{\rightarrow} \alpha \\
  \mu  & ::=  & [\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n] & n \geq 0\\
\end{array}
and the judgements:
\[
 \Gamma \vdash^k M : \alpha
\]

#+reveal:split

Judgement annotations track the number of interaction applications:
\[
\frac{}{x:[\alpha] \vdash^0 x:\alpha}~\text{ax} \quad\quad
\frac{\Gamma_i \vdash^{k_i} M: \alpha_i~~(i \in I~\text{finite})}{\uplus_{i \in I}\Gamma_i \vdash^{\sum_i k_i} M: [\alpha_i]_{i \in I}}~\text{many}
\]

\[
\frac{\Gamma, x:\mu \vdash^k M: \alpha}{\Gamma \vdash^k \lambda_{\bullet/\circ} x.M: \mu \stackrel{\bullet/\circ}{\rightarrow} \alpha}~\lambda
\]

\[
\frac{\Gamma \vdash^k M: \mu \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \alpha \quad \Delta \vdash^{k'} N: \mu}
{\Gamma \uplus \Delta \vdash^{k+k'} M \bullet N : \alpha}~\text{@-silent}
\]

\[
\frac{\Gamma \vdash^k M: \mu \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \alpha \quad \Delta \vdash^{k'} N: \mu}
{\Gamma \uplus \Delta \vdash^{k+k'+1} M \circ N : \alpha}~\text{@-interact}
\]
(plus rules exchanging \(\bullet\) with \(\circ\)).

The /annotated semantics/ \(\csem{M}\) is the set of typings \((\Gamma, k, \alpha)\) with \(\Gamma \vdash^k M : \alpha\).

Erasing colours and indices gives back the relational model \(\sem{M}\).

** Correctness of the semantics

With the same techniques as for the regular relational semantics, we can prove
#+begin_theorem
For any term \(M\) of the checkers calculus:
 - If \(M \Downarrow_{\interact}^k\) then there is a typing \(\Gamma \vdash^k M: \alpha\)
 - If there is a typing \(\Gamma \vdash^k M: \alpha\) then \(M \Downarrow_{\interact}^{k'}\) for some \(k' \leq k\).
#+end_theorem

As a corollary,
\[
\text{if}~\csem{M} \subseteq \csem{N}~\text{then}~M \leimp N.
\]

#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag appear
But inclusion of \(\csem{-}\) is still not what we want: it fails for \(\lambda_\bullet x. \lambda_\bullet y. x \bullet y \leimp \lambda_\bullet x. x\).

** Whiter and cheaper

We write \(\alpha \leq_k^+ \alpha'\) if \(\alpha\) can be obtained from \(\alpha'\) by changing \(k\) /positively occurring/ instances of \(\bullet\) to \(\circ\).

We write \(\alpha \leq_k^- \alpha'\) if \(\alpha\) can be obtained from \(\alpha'\) by changing \(k\) /negatively occurring/ instances of \(\bullet\) to \(\circ\).

For example
\[
 [a] \stackrel{\circ}{\rightarrow} a \leq_1^+ [a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a
\]
\[
 [[a] \stackrel{\circ}{\rightarrow} a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a \leq_1^- [[a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a
 \]

#+reveal:split

#+begin_definition
For closed checkers terms \(M\), \(N\), we write \(M \lepwc N\) if and only if
\[
\text{if}~\vdash^k M: \alpha~\text{then there exists}~\vdash^{k'} N:\alpha'~\text{and}~d~\text{such that}~\alpha' \leq_d^+ \alpha, k'+d \leq k.
\]
#+end_definition

The typing of \(N\) is \(d\)-whiter and at least \(d\)-cheaper.

(The definition extends to terms with free variables in a natural way.)

It remains to prove that \(M \lepwc N\) implies \(M \leimp N\).

* Compositionality

Compositionality is the key!

Suppose the \(\lepwc\) ordering is preserved by term constructors, that is, \(M \lepwc N\) implies \(C[M] \lepwc C[N]\) for any context \(C[-]\).

If \(C[M] \Downarrow_{\interact}^k\) then \(C[M]\) has a typing \(\vdash^k C[M] : \alpha\).

By \(\lepwc\) we obtain a typing \(\vdash^{k'} C[N]:\alpha'\) with \(k' \leq k\).

Hence \(C[N]\Downarrow_{\interact}^{k''}\) for some \(k'' \leq k' \leq k\).

So \(M \leimp N\).

#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
But is \(\lepwc\) compositional?

** The compositionality challenge

Consider an application
\[
 \frac{\vdash^k M: [\alpha] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \beta \quad \vdash^{k'} N: \alpha}{\vdash^{k+k'} M \bullet N: \beta}
\]

If we replace \(N\) by an improved (whiter-cheaper) term
\[
 \vdash^{k''} N': \alpha'
\]
where \(\alpha' \leq_d^+ \alpha\), we can no longer apply the rule.

We'd like to find a new typing for \(M\) of the form
\[
\vdash^{l} M: [\alpha'] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \beta
\]

** Repainting typings

#+begin_theorem
Suppose \(\vdash^k M : \alpha\) and \(\alpha' \leq_1^- \alpha\). Then there exists a typing
\[
 \vdash^{k'} M: \alpha''
\]
such that one of the following holds:
 - \(k = k'\) and \(\alpha'' \leq_1^+ \alpha'\)
 - \(\alpha'' = \alpha'\) and \(|k' - k| = 1\)
#+end_theorem

Whitening an input either forces an output to whiten, or is absorbed by an interaction step appearing or disappearing.

** Termination, and compositionality

In any context, we can replace a term \(M\) by a whiter-cheaper term \(N\) and /repaint/ the type derivation of \(C[M]\) to construct a new type derivation for \(C[N]\). 

Repaintings propagate throughout a type derivation as shown. The process always terminates:

 - every non-final step just changes one \(\bullet\) to \(\circ\) 
 - there are a finite number of annotations in a type

So we get:   
#+begin_theorem
If \(M \lepwc N\) then \(C[M] \lepwc C[N]\) for any context \(C[-]\) and hence \(M \leimp N\).
#+end_theorem

* Summary
We introduced an /interaction-based improvement order/ on checkers terms, and hence on \lambda-terms.

We developed an /annotated relational semantics/ and associated denotational improvement ordering \(\lepwc\).

On \lambda-terms, the order induced by the ordinary relational model coincides with interaction improvement and \(\lepwc\): a /quantitative/ analogue of the Hyland--Wadsworth theorem.

#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
So what does the relational model count? Not reduction steps, but /interactions/ between a term and its context.
