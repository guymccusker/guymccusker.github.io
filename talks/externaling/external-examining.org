* The role of External Examiner

#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
 - A network of QA between universities which autonomously award degrees
 - Overseeing quality and standards

** What it involves

#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
 - Appointments for 2 -- 4 years to oversee one or more degree programmes
 - QA and feedback on assessments throughout the year
 - Review of marking and QA processes
 - Attendance at exam boards to oversee process
 - Discussion with students
 - Writing an annual report

** In the old days...

Pre-pandemic, a great deal of the work was done in person.

#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
Typically visit the university two or three times in the year:
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
 - reviewing exam papers and a selection of coursework assignments
 - reviewing marking of exams and projects
 - attending exam board
#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
On at least one of these occasions there would be a meeting with students.

** In today's world


#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
 - You'll be given access to Moodle or equivalent and expected to review everything
 - This is not practical!
 - So pick and choose. Remember that in the past it was all done in a few days.
 - Seek guidance from the relevant Director of Teaching or equivalent


#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
Around one week of work seems a reasonable estimate.

* Am I qualified to do this?

If you've taught a course and attended an exam board, then yes!

#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
 - Focus on /process/ over content where you don't know the topics in detail
   - are the setting, checking and marking processes appropriate?
   - are they being followed?
   - what happens when something goes wrong?
 - Sometimes you'll spot obvious things:
   - assignments solvable by AI
   - 90% of students scoring 80% or more on a unit, or everyone failing
   - project double-marking not being done in good faith
 - And you do have subject expertise!

* Why would I do this?

#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
 - Helping out a friend's department and the sector
 - Seeing how other universities work is interesting and can be useful
 - Sharing ideas
 - Growing your academic network 
 - [and they pay you]
