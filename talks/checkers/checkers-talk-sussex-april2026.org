* Denotational semantics and observations

This talk is about characterizing the preorder on \lambda-terms induced by a model --- the relational semantics --- as an operationally defined /contextual preorder/.

We'll see that the relational semantics corresponds to a quantitative /improvement order/. 


** Operational semantics
Reduction relation:
\[
 M \rightarrow N~\text{and its transitive closure}~M \rightarrow^{*} N
\]

Use this to define a notion of /observation/ \(\obs{M}\).

Examples:
 - \(M \rightarrow^* 0\) in some typed language with constants
 - \(M \rightarrow^* \mathsf{hnf}\)  (aka \(M\) is /solvable/ in untyped \lambda-calculus)

From this we define a contextual preorder: \(M \lectx N\) means
\[
\text{for all contexts}~C[-]. \obs{C[M]} \subseteq \obs{C[N]}
\]

** Denotational semantics

Interpret programs as mathematical entities:

for a program phrase \(M\), its interpretation is \(\sem{M}\).

Typically \(\sem{M}\) is some kind of function
 - continuous functions between complete partial orders
 - homomorphisms of some algebraic structure

We'll mostly consider the case where \(\sem{M}\) is a /relation/.

Other kinds of models exist, e.g. game semantics. 

** Denotational preorder

If the universe of interpretations carries a natural partial order, we obtain a /denotational/ preorder on programs:
\[
M \leden N \Leftrightarrow \sem{M} \sqsubseteq \sem{N}. 
\]

** Observations in denotational semantics

Typically, denotational models also allow us to make "observations": for example
 - denotational evaluation to values e.g. \(0 \in \sem{M}\)
 - denotational /definedness/: \(\sem{M} \not = \perp\)

We usually expect that the denotational partial order preserves observations i.e.
\[
 \sem{M} \sqsubseteq \sem{N} \Rightarrow \obs{\sem{M}} \subseteq \obs{\sem{N}}. 
\]

** Operational/denotational correspondence properties

*** Observational soundness

\[
 \obs{M} \subseteq \obs{\sem{M}}
\]

Typically easy to prove. It sometimes follows readily from  /reduction invariance/
\[
 M \rightarrow N \quad \Rightarrow \quad \sem{M} = \sem{N}. 
\]
plus simple facts about the semantics of normal forms. 

*** Computational adequacy

\[
\obs{\sem{M}} \subseteq \obs{M}. 
\]

This says that the operational semantics is /adequate/ to generate all observations predicted by the model.

*** Inequational soundness

\[
 M \leden N \Rightarrow M \lectx N. 
\]

This follows from the observational soundness and adequacy properties, provided the denotational semantics is /compositional/ and /monotone/.

*** Completeness

\[
 M \lectx N \Rightarrow M \leden N 
\]

This property is notoriously difficult to establish for many kinds of programming language. For PCF this is the famous /full abstraction problem/.

In the \lambda-calculus, the situation is different.

* B\ouml{}hm preorder up to extensionality
We also define two extensions:
 -  \(M \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}\eta} N\) means that the B\ouml{}hm tree of \(M\) is contained in that of \(N\) /up to \eta-conversion/
 -  \(M \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}\eta^{\text{red}}} N\) means that the B\ouml{}hm tree of \(M\) is contained in that of \(N\) /up to \eta-reduction/

\[
 x \not \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}} \lambda y. xy \quad  \lambda y. xy \not \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}} x
\]
\[
 x \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}\eta} \lambda y. xy \quad  \lambda y. xy  \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}\eta} x
\]
\[
 x \not \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}\eta^{\text{red}}} \lambda y. xy \quad  \lambda y. xy \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}\eta^{\text{red}}} x
\]




** \(D_\infty\) models

In the standard domain-theoretic models of \lambda-calculus -- the so-called \(D_\infty\) models -- we have the correspondence
\[
 \sem{M} \not = \bot \Leftrightarrow M\rightarrow^*\mathsf{hnf}. 
\]

Taking a contextual preorder based on observing reduction to head normal form, we get
\[
 M \leden N \Rightarrow M \lectx N. 
\]

#+reveal:split
We also have
\[
 M \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}\eta} N \Rightarrow M \leden N. 
\]

The "B\ouml{}hm-out" technique ([cite/t/c:@HylandJME:synces]) builds contexts that distinguish terms with distinct B\ouml{}hm-trees:
\[
 M \not \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}\eta} N \Rightarrow \exists C[-]. C[M] \rightarrow^*\mathsf{hnf}~\text{but}~C[N]\not\rightarrow^*\mathsf{hnf}
\]

Therefore
\[
 \sem{M} \sqsubseteq \sem{N} \Leftrightarrow M \lectx N. 
\]

* Relational semantics

This talk is about the /relational semantics/ of the \lambda-calculus.

Relational semantics grew from Girard's /quantitative semantics/ [cite/na:@GirardJY:norfps]
and can be seen as a simple model of linear logic [cite/na:@GirardJY:linl]. 

**  A Cartesian closed category of relations

Our model will interpret programs as certain kinds of /relations/. They can be seen as morphisms of a cartesian closed category defined by:
 - objects: sets
 - morphisms \(A\rightarrow B\): relations from \(\mathbf{M}_f(A)\) to \(B\)

Here \(\mathbf{M}_f(A)\) is the set of /finite multisets/ drawn from \(A\).

#+reveal:split

Given relations 
\[
f : \mathbf{M}_f A \rightarrow B \quad\quad  g : \mathbf{M}_f B \rightarrow C
\]
we can compose like this: $g \circ f$ is 
\[ 
  \{ (S_1 \uplus \ldots \uplus S_n,c) \mid (S_i,b_i) \in f, ([b_1,\ldots,b_n],c) \in g \}
\]

However, the model is easier to explain in the form of a /non-idempotent intersection type/ system.

** Relational semantics as a type system

Idea: give a system of /type derivations/ yielding judgements of the form
\[
 x_1: \mu_1, \ldots, x_n:\mu_n \vdash M: \alpha
\]
The denotation of a term is the set of its valid typings.

In this case, each "type" \(\mu_i\) is a multiset of types, so we get something akin to the morphisms in the category described above.

** Intersection types

\begin{array}{rcll}
  \alpha  & ::=  & a \mid \mu \rightarrow \alpha \\
  \mu  & ::=  & [\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n] & n \geq 0\\
\end{array}

\[
\frac{}{x:[\alpha] \vdash x:\alpha}~\text{ax} \quad\quad
\frac{\Gamma_i \vdash M: \alpha_i~~(i \in I~\text{finite})}{\uplus_{i \in I}\Gamma_i \vdash M: [\alpha_i]_{i \in I}}~\text{many}
\]

\[
\frac{\Gamma, x:\mu \vdash M: \alpha}{\Gamma \vdash \lambda x.M: \mu \rightarrow \alpha}~\lambda
\]

\[
\frac{\Gamma \vdash M: \mu \rightarrow \alpha \quad \Delta \vdash N: \mu}
{\Gamma \uplus \Delta \vdash MN : \alpha}~\text{@}
\]

** Facts about the type system

- A term may have many types: the types are /elements of the denotational semantics/ rather than traditional types
- The @ and /many/ rules together implement the relational composition we defined above
- A term in head normal form trivially has a type. For example
  \[
   x: [] \rightarrow [] \rightarrow a \vdash x M N : a
  \]
  Note that a term such as \( x \Omega\) has a type, though \(\Omega\) does not. 
- Subject reduction and expansion hold

** Subject reduction / expansion

If \( M \rightarrow_\beta N \) then
\[
 \Gamma \vdash M: \alpha \Leftrightarrow \Gamma \vdash N:\alpha
\]

Taking \(\sem{M}\) to be the set of typings of \(M\), this says:
\[
\text{if}~M\rightarrow_\beta N~\text{then}~\sem{M} = \sem{N}. 
\]

** Quantitative aspects

This model has /quantitative/ content: the multisets count something. But what?

The multisets keep track of how many times an argument is used: for example we can type
\[
f: [[\alpha] \rightarrow [\alpha'] \rightarrow \alpha] \vdash \lambda x. f~x~x:[\alpha, \alpha']\rightarrow \alpha.
\]

The /many/ rule means that these multiplicities are also seen in /derivations/. 

\[
 \cfrac{\cfrac{\vdots}{f: [[\alpha] \rightarrow [\alpha'] \rightarrow \alpha] \vdash \lambda x. f~x~x:[\alpha, \alpha']\rightarrow \alpha}\quad \cfrac{\vdash M: \alpha \quad \vdash M: \alpha'}{\vdash M: [\alpha,\alpha']}}{f: [[\alpha] \rightarrow [\alpha'] \rightarrow \alpha]\vdash (\lambda x. f~x~x) M: \alpha }~@
\]

The function uses \(M\) twice, so we need /two/ derivations of \(M\).

Though substitution may duplicate or discard /terms/, it does not duplicate or discard their /type derivations/.

** Quantitative subject reduction

Type derivations track more quantitative information than the types: a typing of \((\lambda x. M)N\) contains /exactly one more @-rule/ than the matching typing of \(M[N/x]\).

So for /head reductions/
\[
 \lambda x_1 \ldots x_n. R~M_1 \ldots M_k  \rightarrow_h \lambda x_1 \ldots x_n. R'~M_1 \ldots M_k 
\]
we have a /quantitative subject reduction/ theorem. 

#+begin_theorem
If \(M \rightarrow_h N\) and \(\Gamma \vdash M:\alpha\) with a typing derivation containing \(k\) instances of the @-rule, then \(\Gamma \vdash N:\alpha\) with a derivation containing \(k-1\) instances of the @-rule.
#+end_theorem

The restriction to head reductions is essential : for example \(x \Omega \rightarrow_\beta x \Omega\), but this is never a head reduction. 

#+begin_theorem
A term \(M\) has a head normal form if and only if it has a type in this system. Therefore
\[
\sem{M} \not = \emptyset \Leftrightarrow M \rightarrow^* \mathsf{hnf}. 
\]
#+end_theorem

** Understanding the relational preorder

We now know:
 \[ \text{if}~\sem{M} \subseteq \sem{N}~\text{then for all}~C[-]. C[M]\rightarrow^* \mathsf{hnf} \Rightarrow C[N]\rightarrow^* \mathsf{hnf}\]
and hence the denotational order implies the extensional B\ouml{}hm-tree order 
\[ M \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}\eta} N\]

The converse is not true. For example \(x \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}\eta} \lambda y. x y \) but
\[
 x : [a] \vdash x: a \quad   x : [a] \not\vdash \lambda y. x y: a \quad 
\]

#+reveal: split

In fact thanks to [Breuvart] we know that

- \(\sem{M} \subseteq \sem{N}\) \Leftrightarrow \(M \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}\eta^{\text{red}}} N\)

But these are all /qualitative/. Where have the quantities gone? Can we recover a contextual ordering by paying attention to quantities?

* Improvement orders

Sands introduced the idea of a contextual /improvement/ order.

Idea: observe not just what happens but how long it takes.

For example: observe the /length/ of a head-reduction sequence.

We write \( M \Downarrow^k \) if \(M\) reduces to hnf in \(k\) head reduction steps.

Then we can define /improvement/: \(M \leimp N \) iff 
\[
 \text{for all contexts}~C[-]. C[M]\Downarrow^k \Rightarrow \exists k'\leq k. C[N]\Downarrow^{k'}
\]

\(N\) does the same things as \(M\) in fewer steps.

#+reveal:split

Note that an order that counts reductions /cannot/ be the order induced by a denotational semantics that is sound for beta-reduction.

So our plan to use this idea to capture the relational order must fail. Yet here we are...

** Checkers syntax

Terms are labelled with two badges: \(\bullet\) for "internal" and \(\circ\) for "external".
\[
 M, N ::= x \mid \lambda_\bullet x. M \mid \lambda_\circ x. M \mid M \bullet N \mid M \circ N
\]

Silent reductions:
\[
 (\lambda_\bullet x.M)\bullet N \rightarrow M[N/x]  \quad  (\lambda_\circ x.M)\circ N \rightarrow M[N/x] 
\]

Interaction reductions:
\[
 (\lambda_\bullet x.M)\circ N \stackrel{\interact}{\rightarrow} M[N/x]  \quad  (\lambda_\circ x.M)\bullet N \stackrel{\interact}{\rightarrow} M[N/x] 
\]

We can embed the ordinary \lambda-calculus by using \(\bullet\) for every label. Every reduction would be silent.

The fun starts when we embed a \(\bullet\)-labelled term in a \(\circ\)-labelled context. 

** Example

Two versions of the identity function in the context \(- \circ  (\lambda_\circ x.x) \).



\begin{eqnarray*}
(\lambda_\bullet x. x) \circ (\lambda_\circ x.x) & \intred & \lambda_\circ x.x
\end{eqnarray*}

#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
\begin{eqnarray*}
 (\lambda_\bullet x. \lambda_\bullet y. x \bullet y) \circ (\lambda_\circ x.x) & \intred & \lambda_\bullet y. (\lambda_\circ x.x) \bullet y \\
 & \intred & \lambda_\bullet y. y
\end{eqnarray*}

#+ATTR_REVEAL: :frag (appear)
Note that eta-expansion increases the number of interaction steps, and also changes the labelling of the result. 

** Checkers improvement ordering

We can now define an appropriate improvement ordering for terms of this calculus.

We observe the /number of interaction steps/ in a head-reduction sequence.

We write \( M \Downarrow^k \) if \(M\) reduces to hnf in \(k\) head interaction steps.

Then we can define /improvement/: \(M \leimp N \) iff 
\[
 \text{for all contexts}~C[-]. C[M]\Downarrow^k \Rightarrow \exists k'\leq k. C[N]\Downarrow^{k'}
\]

\(N\) does the same things as \(M\) in fewer interaction steps.

#+reveal:split

For a regular \lambda-term labelled with \(\bullet\) throughout, /all steps are silent/.

Terms that are \beta-equivalent will be equivalent in this preorder.

But our example shows that \(\circ\)-labelled contexts can distinguish between \eta-convertible terms: \eta-reduction seems to deliver improvement.

*Conjecture* The interaction improvement preorder on \lambda-terms coincides with the relational preorder.

*First step* If \(M \leimp N\) then \(M \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}\eta^{\text{red}}} N\) (and hence \(\sem{M} \subseteq \sem{N}\) in the relational semantics). 

Proved via syntactic methods akin to Hyland's B\ouml{}hm-out technique.

For the converse we will refine the relational semantics. 

* Checkers semantics

We annotate the types:
\begin{array}{rcll}
  \alpha  & ::=  & a \mid \mu \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \alpha \mid \mu \stackrel{\circ}{\rightarrow} \alpha \\
  \mu  & ::=  & [\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n] & n \geq 0\\
\end{array}
and the judgements:
\[
 \Gamma \vdash^k M : \alpha
\]

#+reveal:split

Judgement annotations track the number of interaction applications:
\[
\frac{}{x:[\alpha] \vdash^0 x:\alpha}~\text{ax} \quad\quad
\frac{\Gamma_i \vdash^{k_i} M: \alpha_i~~(i \in I~\text{finite})}{\uplus_{i \in I}\Gamma_i \vdash^{\sum_i k_i} M: [\alpha_i]_{i \in I}}~\text{many}
\]

\[
\frac{\Gamma, x:\mu \vdash^k M: \alpha}{\Gamma \vdash^k \lambda_{\bullet/\circ} x.M: \mu \stackrel{\bullet/\circ}{\rightarrow} \alpha}~\lambda
\]

\[
\frac{\Gamma \vdash^k M: \mu \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \alpha \quad \Delta \vdash^{k'} N: \mu}
{\Gamma \uplus \Delta \vdash^{k+k'} M \bullet N : \alpha}~\text{@-silent}
\]

\[
\frac{\Gamma \vdash^k M: \mu \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \alpha \quad \Delta \vdash^{k'} N: \mu}
{\Gamma \uplus \Delta \vdash^{k+k'+1} M \circ N : \alpha}~\text{@-interact}
\]

(and vice versa). 

** Examples

In the plain relational semantics we have the following typings for identity-like terms
\[
 \vdash \lambda x. x: [[a] \rightarrow a] \rightarrow [a] \rightarrow a
\]
\[
 \vdash \lambda x. \lambda y. x y: [[a] \rightarrow a] \rightarrow [a] \rightarrow a
\]

In the checkers calculus we have:
\[
 \vdash^0 \lambda_\bullet x. x : [[a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} [a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a
 \quad\quad
 \vdash^0 \lambda_\bullet x. \lambda_\bullet y. x \bullet y :
 [[a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} [a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a
\]
\[
 \vdash^0 \lambda_\bullet x. x : [[a] \stackrel{\circ}{\rightarrow} a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} [a] \stackrel{\circ}{\rightarrow} a
 \quad\quad
\vdash^1 \lambda_\bullet x. \lambda_\bullet y. x \bullet y :
 [[a] \stackrel{\circ}{\rightarrow} a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} [a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a
\]

Key insight: \eta-reductions lead to typings that are either the same, or "whiter and cheaper". 

** Correctness of the semantics

With the same techniques as for the regular relational semantics, we can prove
#+begin_theorem
For any term \(M\) of the checkers calculus:
 - If \(M \Downarrow^k\) then there is a typing \(\Gamma \vdash^k M: \alpha\)
 - If there is a typing \(\Gamma \vdash^k M: \alpha\) then \(M \Downarrow^{k'}\) for some \(k' \leq k\). 
#+end_theorem

As a corollary,
\[
\text{if}~\sem{M} \subseteq \sem{N}~\text{then}~M \leimp N. 
\]

However, semantic inclusion does not precisely capture \(\leimp\) because we know
\[
\lambda_\bullet x. \lambda_\bullet y. x \bullet y \leimp \lambda_\bullet x. x
\]

We need to capture the "whiter and cheaper" idea.

** Seeing improvements in typings

We write \(\alpha \leq_k^+ \alpha'\) if \(\alpha\) can be obtained from \(\alpha'\) by changing \(k\) /positively occurring/ instances of \(\bullet\) to \(\circ\).

We write \(\alpha \leq_k^- \alpha'\) if \(\alpha\) can be obtained from \(\alpha'\) by changing \(k\) /negatively occurring/ instances of \(\bullet\) to \(\circ\).

For example
\[
 [a] \stackrel{\circ}{\rightarrow} a \leq_1^+ [a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a
\]
\[
 [[a] \stackrel{\circ}{\rightarrow} a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a \leq_1^- [[a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a
 \]

#+begin_definition
For closed checkers terms \(M\), \(N\), we write \(M \lepwc N\) if and only if
\[
\text{if}~\vdash^k M: \alpha~\text{then there exists}~\vdash^{k'} N:\alpha'~\text{and}~d~\text{such that}~\alpha' \leq_d^+ \alpha, k'+d \leq k. 
\]
#+end_definition

The typing of \(N\) is \(d\)-whiter and at least \(d\)-cheaper.

** From the improvement order order to \(\lepwc\)

For ordinary \lambda-terms, we know
\[
 M \leimp N \Rightarrow M \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}\eta^{\text{red}}} N
\]

By direct analysis of typing derivations on B\ouml{}hm-trees we can prove
#+begin_theorem
If \(M \sqsubseteq_{\mathbf{B}\eta^{\text{red}}} N\) then \(M \lepwc N\). 
#+end_theorem

It remains to prove that \(M \lepwc N\) implies \(M \leimp N\).

** Is \(\lepwc\) compositional?

Suppose the \(\lepwc\) ordering is preserved by term constructors.

Then \(M \lepwc N\) implies \(C[M] \lepwc C[N]\) for any context \(C[-]\).

If \(C[M] \Downarrow^k\) then \(C[M]\) has a typing \(\vdash^k C[M] : \alpha\).

By \(\lepwc\) we obtain a typing \(\vdash^{k'} C[N]:\alpha'\) with \(k' \leq k\).

Hence \(C[N]\Downarrow^{k''}\) for some \(k'' \leq k' \leq k\).

So \(M \leimp N\).

But is \(\lepwc\) compositional?

** The challenge of compositionality

Consider an application
\[
 \frac{\vdash^k M: [\alpha] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \beta \quad \vdash^{k'} N: \alpha}{\vdash^{k+k'} M \bullet N: \beta}
\]

If we replace \(N\) by an improved term
\[
 \vdash^{k''} N': \alpha'
\]
where \(\alpha' \leq_d^+ \alpha\), we can no longer apply the rule.

We'd like to find a new typing for \(M\) of the form
\[
\vdash^{l} M: [\alpha'] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \beta
\]

** Repainting typings

#+begin_theorem
Suppose \(\vdash^k M : \alpha\) and \(\alpha' \leq_1^- \alpha\). Then there exists a typing
\[
 \vdash^{k'} M: \alpha''
\]
such that one of the following holds:
 - \(k = k'\) and \(\alpha'' \leq_1^+ \alpha'\)
 - \(\alpha'' = \alpha'\) and \(|k' - k| = 1\)

#+end_theorem

** Repainting: easy case

Start with
\[
 \vdash^0 \lambda_\bullet x. x : [[a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} [a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a
\]
Change a negatively occurring \(\bullet\) to \(\circ\):
\[
 \not\vdash^0 \lambda_\bullet x. x : [[a] \stackrel{\circ}{\rightarrow} a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} [a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} a
\]
Repainting a positively occurring \(\bullet\) to \(\circ\) yields a valid typing:
\[
 \vdash^0 \lambda_\bullet x. x : [[a] \stackrel{\circ}{\rightarrow} a] \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} [a] \stackrel{\circ}{\rightarrow} a
\]

** Repainting: propagation to interactions

As we repaint a typing derivation, we may encounter
\[
 \frac{\vdash^k M: \mu \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \alpha \quad \vdash^{k'} N: \mu}{\vdash^{k+k'} M\bullet N: \alpha}
\]
being repainted to
\[
 \frac{\vdash^k M: \mu \stackrel{\circ}{\rightarrow} \alpha \quad \vdash^{k'} N: \mu}{\not\vdash^{k+k'} M\bullet N: \alpha}
\]
The repainting here propagates to an additional interaction step:
\[
 \frac{\vdash^k M: \mu \stackrel{\circ}{\rightarrow} \alpha \quad \vdash^{k'} N: \mu}{\vdash^{k+k'+1} M\bullet N: \alpha}
\]

** Repainting: propagation back and forth
We may need to repaint more steps in the derivation: given
\[
 \frac{\vdash^k M: \mu \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \alpha \quad \vdash^{k'} N: \mu}{\vdash^{k+k'} M\bullet N: \alpha}
\]
Repaint \(N:\mu\) to \(\mu' \leq_1^+ \mu\):
\[
 \frac{\vdash^k M: \mu \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \alpha \quad \vdash^{k'} N: \mu'}{\not\vdash^{k+k'} M\bullet N: \alpha}
\]
repainting propagates to \(M\):
\[
 \frac{\not\vdash^k M: \mu' \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \alpha \quad \vdash^{k'} N: \mu'}{\not\vdash^{k+k'} M\bullet N: \alpha}
\]
which requires repainting \(\mu'\) to \(\mu'' \leq_1^- \mu'\):
\[
 \frac{\vdash^k M: \mu'' \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \alpha \quad \vdash^{k'} N: \mu'}{\not\vdash^{k+k'} M\bullet N: \alpha}
\]
#+reveal: split
This is still not valid! But the process may terminate at the next step with e.g. 
\[
 \frac{\vdash^k M: \mu'' \stackrel{\bullet}{\rightarrow} \alpha \quad \vdash^{k''} N: \mu''}{\vdash^{k+k''} M\bullet N: \alpha}
\]

** Termination of repainting

The termination argument is very easy:

 - every non-final step changes one \(\bullet\) to \(\circ\) and does not change the type in any other way
 - there are a finite number of annotations in a type

** \(\lepwc\) is compositional

#+begin_theorem
If \(M \lepwc N\) then \(C[M] \lepwc C[N]\) for any context \(C[-]\) and hence \(M \leimp N\). 
#+end_theorem

* Summary

We introduced an /interaction based improvement order/ on checkers terms, and hence on \lambda-terms.

We developed an /annotated relational semantics/ and associated denotational improvement ordering \(\lepwc\). 

On \lambda-terms, we showed that the order induced by the ordinary relational model coincides with the improvement order, yielding a /quantitative/ analogue of the Hyland-Wadsworth theorem.
